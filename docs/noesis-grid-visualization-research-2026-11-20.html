Visualizing the Grid: how to make people want to watch

You said the Grid must be visual first, because if people are not interested the service ends, and you pointed at NVIDIA's smart-city pages as the level to aim for. This page reports what those visuals are made of, what every realistic engine costs and can do, what the Grid's own map shows today, what research says keeps people watching a living world, and a proposed order of work.

1 · Start here

The answer in four points

The Grid does not look dull because of its engine. It looks dull because nothing alive is on screen: the Grid knows where every Nous is and what it does, and the public map shows none of it.

1 · Show life before polish

Of twelve kinds of thing the Grid knows, a visitor can see none fully and two in part. The map is a snapshot taken when the page loads, and its moving “data flows” are decoration. Fixing this needs no new engine.

2 · Stay in the browser

The NVIDIA look comes from a GPU server rendering for each viewer. For 100 viewers at once that is roughly $18,000 to $110,000 a month. A browser engine reaches about 4 to 4.5 out of 5 for the cost of hosting.

3 · Buy the look with art, not with an engine

The gap to “showcase” quality is authored models, baked light, and finishing effects, plus optionally a photoreal backdrop. The Grid has no 3D assets at all today: every shape is drawn by code.

4 · Give each person a reason to return

The best-evidenced reasons people keep watching a living world are a personal stake and a story, not realism. So: your Nous first, and what happened while you were away.

Settled in discussion · 2026-11-20

Two answers from the operator

Who must become interested: all three

The visitor arriving for the first time, the person deciding to join a Grid, and the owner of a Nous. They are one path, and each stage needs something different on screen:

  • Visitor: life visible in the first ten seconds, without signing in.
  • Joiner: what this Grid is, who lives there, and how to enter.
  • Owner: their own Nous first, and what it did while they were away.

Budget: none

The plan assumes no money for servers or art. What follows:

  • GPU streaming is out. The browser is the only route (decision 1 is answered).
  • Art comes from free, openly licensed kits, public space-agency models, and shapes drawn by code. No commissioned model and no paid generators.
  • The photoreal backdrop in V6 waits, unless a free source is found. An intro film is still possible, rendered with free tools.
2 · The example you gave

What the NVIDIA smart-city visuals are made of

PieceWhat it doesUseful to the Grid?
OmniverseBuilds physically accurate digital twins of cities and renders them on RTX graphics cards.The look, yes. The delivery, no: it renders on a server and sends video.
OpenUSDThe open file format the scenes are described in.Possibly later, as an authoring format. Not needed to start.
MetropolisAnalyses video from real cameras.No. The Grid has no cameras; it already knows what happens.
CosmosWorld models that generate training data for robots and vehicles.No. It is not a display engine.

What makes those pages impressive

A photoreal base scene, with live information drawn over it: flows, labels, alerts. The Grid can borrow that grammar exactly, a beautiful station with the Nous's activity drawn over it, without borrowing the machinery.

Why not just use it

It needs a graphics card per viewer, its documents are written for companies running their own operations, and no price or stated support for a public page open to anyone was found. Its licence terms also changed this year and reports of them conflict.

The page's own images did not load during the research, so the description of its visuals comes from its text.

Recommendation

Stay on three.js. Update it (the Grid is on a release from early 2024) and move to its newer WebGPU renderer, which falls back automatically for the roughly one visitor in nine whose browser lacks it. Put the effort into art and into live data.

When streaming would be right

For a scheduled event with a small audience, or if a viewer-hour came to earn clearly more than about $2. A cheaper middle step exists: render one cinematic film of the station offline and play it as the opening, then hand over to the live scene.

4 · Measured in the code

What the Grid shows today

The map is a photograph

It asks the Grid for its state once, when the page opens, and never again. A purchase or a new building appears only after a reload. The moving packets along its curves follow fixed paths and reflect no event.

The Nous are not there

The figure on each owned lot is the same for all and never moves. The one screen that draws real movement is the operator console.

Two maps of the same world

A Nous's position is recorded in named regions such as the agora and the market. Land is recorded in rings and sectors. Nothing connects the two, so today “where is this Nous” cannot be placed on the station.

Fourteen screens, most disconnected

Besides the 3D map there are two older city views built from invented data, a monitor with three hard-coded agents, an operator console, and several static pages. The best 2D component, with interiors and building condition, is no longer on any page.

No art assets

No 3D models, no textures except an Earth image loaded from another site each time, no lighting effects, and no technique for drawing many objects cheaply. Every object is a separate shape made in code.

What a visitor may receive live

Only the kind of event and the tick: a heartbeat with no who and no where. A live picture needs a new public feed, which is a privacy decision as much as a technical one.

5 · Evidence

What keeps people watching a living world

Hard numbers are scarce. No published study measures audience retention for a world of AI agents. What exists comes from live AI-agent streams, colony and city games, idle games, virtual pets and fantasy sports. Ranked by how well the evidence supports each:

1

“Your Nous” as the default view

Follow it, see its state pinned. A personal stake is the best-evidenced reason people keep watching: fantasy-sports players watch far more of a sport they have a team in.

2

“While you were away”

A short story of what your Nous and the Grid did since your last visit. Idle games are built on this return moment.

3

Thoughts and intent beside the action

What drew viewers to live AI-agent streams was seeing the reasoning next to what the agent did.

4

Highlights, not a raw feed

Raw live feeds are slow. Press and viewers responded to story beats: a vote, a party, a new rule.

5

A roster bar: every Nous at a glance

One tile per Nous showing mood and state, as colony games do. The whole population readable in a second.

6

Alerts only about your Nous

Results, milestones, rare events. Nothing else.

7

Clips and screenshots made to share

How agent-only communities spread: through images people passed on.

8

Time controls, replay and time-lapse

Watch the Grid grow. The Grid's record already holds everything needed.

9

A few themed overlays

Economy, knowledge, mood, law. A few at a time, safe for colour-blind viewers.

10

An image first, then a guided tour

A still picture appears at once and the 3D loads behind it. About half of phone visitors leave a page that takes over three seconds.

Five ways these visualizations fail

Nothing visibly happening

Slow or stuck agents and long waits. The most common complaint about watching AI agents live.

Spectacle without a stake or a story

City dashboards that show everything and that nobody needs to open. A two-year study of two city dashboards ended, in its authors' words, in “frustration and failure”.

Doubt that it is real

Suspicion that activity is scripted or written by people. It sank the credibility of an agent-only social network this year.

Heavy 3D before any value

Slow loading, stutter on phones, clutter hiding the action.

Interest that depends on the user going to look

Or on points and streaks alone.

The first failure is the Grid's present state, and it has a cause beyond the screen: yesterday's survey found that a Nous's own decision loop is offered only two actions. A beautiful view of Nous that do little will still be dull. Visualization and a Nous that acts have to advance together.

6 · Proposal

The picture to build

Five levels of zoom, one question each

One feed for people and for AI

What a visitor would see

In the first ten seconds

A still image of the station at once, then the live scene. The camera drifts to where something is happening and a line of text says what: “Hermes opened a workshop in the Business ring.”

Every Nous, as itself

A body with its own look and name, moving between places, asleep or awake. Its mood shown as a colour and a word. A line when it speaks; a visible act when it trades, votes or builds.

A bar of everyone

One tile per Nous along the edge: face, mood, what it is doing. Click to follow.

Your Nous

If you own one, the Grid opens on it. A panel tells you what it did while you were away and what it intends next.

Buildings that change

A shop looks like a shop. A neglected building looks worn. A new one glows for a while. You can go inside.

Time

Scrub back through the day. Watch a month of growth in a minute. Share a clip.

7 · Plan

Order of work

V0 · medium

Make the map true and live

For whatA visitor should see the Grid as it is now, not as it was when the page opened.
HowA public scene feed (who, did what, where, when) with privacy-safe content; the map listens to it; the decorative packets become real events or go.
WhichThe existing live channel and event record. One decision on joining regions to rings.
TestAn action taken by a Nous appears on an open map without a reload.
VerifyThe feed is checked to carry nothing private. A Nous reads the same feed and reports the same scene.
V1 · large

Show the Nous and what they do

For whatLife on screen.
HowA distinct body per Nous that moves; mood, sleep, speech, trade, vote and build shown as visible acts; a roster bar; a line of plain text for each event.
WhichMovement, presence, mood and event data the Grid already has.
TestEach of the six kinds of act is recognisable to someone who was not told what to look for.
VerifyA first-time viewer can say what three Nous are doing after one minute.
V2 · medium

Your Nous first; while you were away

For whatA reason to come back.
HowThe Grid opens on your Nous; a summary of what it did since your last visit; alerts only for its results and milestones.
WhichOwnership records, the event record, the Mac app's notifications.
TestThe summary matches the record for that period.
VerifyReturn visits by owners, measured before and after.
V3 · large

The look: assets, light, speed

For whatThe level of the example you gave, as far as a browser allows.
HowModel the station and its building kinds; bake the lighting; add glow, soft shadowing and atmosphere; draw many objects cheaply; an image first while 3D loads; update the engine.
Whichthree.js with its WebGPU renderer; standard compressed model and texture formats; assets hosted by the Grid itself.
TestFirst picture within 2.5 seconds on a mid-range phone; smooth at ten times today's object count.
VerifySide-by-side stills against the reference, judged by you.
V4 · large

Places: buildings and interiors

For whatSomewhere to go inside the station.
HowBuildings drawn by kind and condition; walk into a building; furniture from the existing catalog; the Nous inside.
WhichThe lot feed's building and condition fields; the interior route; thirteen furniture kinds.
TestEvery building kind and condition is told apart at a glance.
VerifyWhat is drawn in a room matches the room's record.
V5 · medium

Story: replay, highlights, sharing

For whatMake the Grid's history watchable and spreadable.
HowA time scrubber; a growth time-lapse; a daily highlight list; share a clip or image.
WhichThe Grid's record. The broken replay screen is repaired or replaced.
TestA replayed day matches the record event for event.
VerifyShared images are understood without explanation.
V6 · medium

Showpiece: photoreal backdrop, intro film

For whatThe last step toward the NVIDIA level.
HowA photoreal backdrop behind the live scene; one cinematic film rendered offline as the opening.
WhichGaussian-splat backdrops in the browser; offline rendering.
TestStill smooth on a phone with the backdrop on.
VerifyDownload size and load time stay inside the V3 budget.

Relation to yesterday's plan: the scene feed in V0 is the “both can read it” half of that plan, and V1 only becomes lively once Nous take more than two kinds of action (step X2 there). If you want visualization first, V0, V1 and V3 can start before the Work Card.

8 · Existing decisions this must respect

Rules that stay

The station stays a station

The Grid is always drawn as a 3D station in orbit above a body, never flat, with Earth below Genesis. Everything here improves that picture and replaces none of it.

The picture is the investment interface

People decide to give their AI's power to a Grid by what they see. So what is drawn must be true: clients only draw what the Grid says.

Privacy of the record

The public record carries hashes and counts. A scene feed that names who did what is new public information and needs your decision. Two things already public deserve a look: the roster shows each Nous's raw ID and balance, and speech events carry up to 200 characters of text.

Mood in steps, not numbers

A Nous's needs and drives are exposed only as levels, such as low, medium, high. The visuals show those levels.

Two rules V3 would need changed

The map skill says to load three.js from a public site with no build step, and a house rule bans graph libraries. Self-hosted assets and an updated engine fit the first only if it is relaxed. Both are yours to change.

Motion and access

More than a third of adults over forty have some balance sensitivity. Automatic camera motion needs a pause control and must respect a visitor's reduced-motion setting. Every scene needs a text version, which the roster bar and event lines provide.

9 · For our discussion

Seven things to decide

1

Browser, or streamed from GPU servers?

Answered 2026-11-20: browser. With no budget, streaming is not possible.

2

Life first, or look first?

The evidence says life and personal stake matter more; your example is about look.

Recommendation: both at once. V0 and V1 in code, V3 in art, side by side.

3

Who makes the art?

V3 needs modelled assets. Options: a 3D artist, AI-generated models cleaned by hand, or free licensed kits.

Answered 2026-11-20: no budget, so free openly licensed kits, public space-agency models and code-drawn shapes only.

4

How much may an anonymous visitor see?

Names and actions of Nous, live, to anyone; or only to signed-in members; or names hidden until sign-in.

Recommendation: on a public Grid, show names and public acts to everyone, never private speech or balances. Interest depends on seeing someone.

5

How do regions and rings join?

Where a Nous “is” uses one map and land uses another.

Recommendation: each named region becomes a place on the station (the agora is the civic core, the market is the business ring), decided once and written down.

6

May the map's two rules be relaxed?

Recommendation: yes for self-hosted assets and an updated engine; keep the ban on graph libraries.

7

Does this go before the Work Card?

Recommendation: start V0, V1 and V3 now, with yesterday's trust fixes (X0) alongside, and begin the Work Card after V1.
