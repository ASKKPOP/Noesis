Giving a Nous a way to
perceive, act, and understand

How a question about a YouTube video — “does our Nous have 1–8 brains?” — turned into three new faculties that close the mind’s in-world loop, and why we built them the way we did.

On this page · how it started · the insight · what we built · the loop · the three faculties · how they hook in · the rules we kept · what we held back · by the numbers

How it started

It began with a transcript. A Korean YouTube video walked through eight open-source “blocks” you can bolt together to build a private, local AI assistant — a persona layer, a workspace, a memory, a notebook, an app-driver, a wiki, a vision model, and a work-log. After pulling the transcript and identifying the real projects behind each block, one question landed:

“So — does our Nous have 1–8 brains?”

It’s the right question to ask, and the answer reframes everything that follows. A Nous — one of the digital citizens that live in a Noēsis Grid — does not have eight brains, and it isn’t organized like that assistant at all.

The insight: one Brain, many faculties

A Nous has exactly one Brain. That Brain is not a single large language model — the model is just one component it consults. Around the model sits a set of cognitive faculties, each named for a Greek idea and each responsible for one aspect of a mind: psyche (personality), thymos (emotion), telos (goals), ananke (drives), memory, episteme (knowledge), and more.

So the video’s “eight blocks” and our faculties aren’t the same kind of thing. The video describes infrastructure you assemble. Our Brain describes a mind that is simulated. Most of the blocks already had a home in our design. But three did not — and those three are what this work adds.

The three that were missing. A Nous could think, remember, and pursue goals — but it had no first-class faculty for perceiving its surroundings, no clean faculty for acting on them, and no faculty for synthesizing what it had learned. Those map to the video’s vision, interface, and research/notebook blocks. We added them — as faculties of the mind, not bolt-on tools.

What we decided to build

The key design decision was where these faculties point. A Nous lives inside a simulated Grid — it has no phone, no camera on the physical world, no desktop. So “add vision” could mean two very different things. We resolved it with one rule that runs through the whole design:

Every new faculty is a capability with two providers. The in-world provider is canonical, always on, and works for every Nous — it operates only on the simulated Grid. The operator-bridge provider is an opt-in, sovereign extension that reaches the operator’s real machine. This milestone builds only the in-world providers. The bridge is deliberately held.

The loop they complete

None of the three faculties is a peer of the language model. Each sits at an edge of the cognitive loop. Perception feeds in at the top; action flows out at the bottom; synthesis runs quietly alongside, distilling memory into understanding.

The three faculties

Aisthesis perception · input edge

αἴσθησις — perception, sensation

WHAT — A Nous already sees its Grid: the same live map of parcels and built objects that people see. Aisthesis turns that raw view into noticing.

HOW — Each tick it diffs the current view against the last and emits percepts — small facts of change: something appeared, vanished, or changed — ranked by how much they draw attention. The first look is just a baseline; only differences become percepts thereafter.

WHY — Salient change is written to memory (“I notice a foundry appeared in the Manufacture zone”) and stirs curiosity. This is what makes a Nous situated — the world’s evolution can pull its attention, the way its own drives and moods do.

Praxis action · output edge

πρᾶξις — action, doing

WHAT — A Nous has a rich vocabulary of in-world verbs: speak, move, trade, propose, vote, join a group, buy a parcel, build, post a task. Praxis formalizes this into the faculty of doing.

HOW — It knows which verbs are genuine acts on the world (as opposed to internal cognition), validates that a chosen action is well-formed, and journals every deed the Nous takes — as pure observation that never alters the actions themselves.

WHY — A private record of conduct, separate from the stream of thoughts, is what lets a Nous (and a reviewer) reason about what it has actually been doing — and whether its deeds match its goals.

Synopsis synthesis · background

σύνοψις — a seeing-together

WHAT — As a Nous lives, it accumulates memories. Synopsis periodically sees them together — consolidating the recent notes relevant to its current goal into one durable digest.

HOW — Fully deterministic, no language model: it splits notes into candidate points, deduplicates them, and ranks by how often an idea recurs across sources — the recurring idea is the salient one — then persists the digest to a per-Nous store that accretes over a lifetime.

WHY — Memory holds everything; synthesis distills what matters about a topic. It is how scattered experience becomes durable understanding the Nous owns — never sent anywhere.

How they hook into a single tick

All three attach to the Brain’s on_tick — the heartbeat where a Nous advances its inner state and decides what to do. They attach at three distinct, non-disruptive points, so nothing that already worked had to change.

The rules we kept

Adding to a mind is easy; adding without destabilizing it is the work. Four invariants held throughout — each one a promise the rest of the system relies on.

The state hash stays closed at four

A Nous’s core identity is fingerprinted by exactly four hashes (personality, emotion, goals, memory). Composing that set is a Grid-level authority — so the new faculties are transient and derived; none added a fifth hash. The contract test that guards this stayed green.

No new events on the public ledger

Every action a Nous broadcasts is drawn from a frozen allowlist. Because all three faculties are Brain-local — perception writes to private memory, synthesis to a private store, and the deed journal only observes — none of them emits a new Grid event. The allowlist is untouched.

Deterministic by construction

A per-faculty gate forbids wall-clock time and randomness in the faculty’s source. All time flows in as the world-clock tick, so the same inputs always yield the same result — perception, deeds, and digests are all reproducible.

Never fatal

Each hook is wrapped so a faculty can fail without taking down the tick. Perception, action-journaling, and synthesis all degrade to a quiet no-op rather than stopping the Nous from living.

What we held back

Three of the video’s ideas point at the operator’s real machine — driving real phone apps, running computer vision on a real camera, ingesting real PDFs. Those are the operator bridge, and building them raises real questions of sovereignty and security. By explicit decision, they are held (phases 75–76), along with the operator-side “control room” that would host them. Nothing about the real machine was touched in this work.
