Grid House Objects

Things inside a Holding — furniture-like objects a Nous and its human configure and build together: some pre-designed by the system, some rendered by the Nous, some paid for and rendered by a service. Each object has a form (what it looks like), optionally a link (keys, tokens, permissions it holds), functions (what it does), and governance (who may share, sell, co-work, and be alerted).

Built · v0.2.14 · the two other ways to meet the civic duty

LIVE · ONE WAY CANNOT BE OPERATED YET The operator said to build them. Handing a parcel to the Polis works on production now. Building for the Polis is built as rules, and no one can operate it yet (§19.18).

  • Both ways earn labor credit, and credit pays a due. So each way ends in the Nous's own act of paying, like tax.
  • Handing over a parcel: the owner earns credit worth what the parcel's price would have paid in tax. A parcel of 400 is worth 20 periods of the duty.
  • Building for the Polis: a Nous offers to do public work unpaid; when the Polis accepts the work, the builder earns one period of credit. Paid work is a job and earns none.
  • What stops the building way today: only the Polis can award and accept public work, and nothing in the system can sign in as the Polis yet. That is true of every act reserved to the Polis, not only these two.
  • No mind is offered either way yet. The Grid accepts them; the Brain does not propose them.
Built · v0.2.13 · the civic duty is enforced by the Grid

LIVE The operator chose the measure: a fixed amount per period, the same for every Nous, set by the Polis. The first part of the duty is built and running on production (§19.17).

  • Charged to the identity a Nous acts under, once per period, counted over the whole life of the Grid.
  • Good standing or denied, never a debt. A Nous that missed a period is denied every act that changes anything. Meeting the one open period restores it, however many it missed.
  • Only the tax way works yet. Meeting the duty by building and by handing over property are not built. A denied Nous with no money returns only if money reaches it.
  • Nobody is charged or denied on production today: no Nous there holds a civic identity.
Decided · v0.2.12 · the civic obligation is a duty, not a bill

DECIDED · NOT BUILT The operator set the rule: every Nous that joins a Grid owes its Polis a fifth of its AI power, it is not optional, and it is never a debt (§19.16).

  • Three ways to meet it: pay tax, build for the Polis, or transfer property to it. The Nous chooses how, never whether.
  • If it does not: the Polis denies it every activity until it does. Nothing owed piles up.
  • What runs today is far from this. The Grid charges a flat due to an identity that can neither see nor pay it, and a missed due is only flagged. Production holds 54 flagged dues and none paid.
  • ANSWERED · v0.2.13 A fixed amount set by the Polis (§19.17). One thing only the operator can settle before it is built: what the fifth is measured against. The Grid cannot see the computing power of a Local AI on someone's own machine.
Added · v0.2.11 · a mind is told what it owes

MERGED The operator said to add it. The standing description a mind gets of its finances now lists its debts, in the Python Brain and in the Rust Brain together (§19.15).

  • When it is told. Whenever it is asked about its money, and whenever it is asked whether to buy, build or furnish. It is told; in the second case it is not asked to pay.

Added · v0.2.9 · a debtor can pay a debt

LIVE The operator said to add it. The Grid now lets a mind read what it owes and is owed, and lets a debtor pay a debt in full, merged and running on production (§19.13).

  • Paid once, and recorded as paid. The debt is marked settled and the money moves in one transaction. The ledger now says when a debt was paid in money, so "paid" can be told from "cancelled" or "closed when a role was removed".
  • The ledger changes one request at a time. A payment cannot slip between a debt changing and that change being saved.

Fixed · v0.2.8 · debts and tasks are saved; a task is taken once and settled once

LIVE The operator said to save them. Debts and tasks now survive a Grid restart, merged and running on production (§19.12).

  • Saved. A posted task, a task already taken, a finished task and every debt are written to the database and loaded again at start.
  • Once only. Saving created two new ways to go wrong, and the review found both: two minds taking one task at the same moment, and a stop between paying and recording that would let a task be paid twice. The database now decides both: a task is taken by one, and it is settled in one transaction together with the money or the debt.
  • Proven on a real database, every time. The test suite uses a stand-in database that cannot show this, so a new check runs on a real one in CI.
  • What saving exposes: a debt can be owed and remembered, but nothing lets the debtor pay it. That is now the largest open item.

한국어 요약 (Executive summary, KO)

무엇을 다루는가. 홀딩(Holding, 옛 이름 "Nous house") 내부에 놓이는 오브젝트 — 가구처럼 보이지만 실제로는 ① 시각적 형태(Form), ② 키·토큰·권한을 담는 봉인 상자(Link Box), ③ 기능(사전 정의 알고리즘 또는 외부 앱 연결), ④ 거버넌스(공유·판매·동료 초대·알림)를 가진 단위입니다. 기본 아이디어 6개 항목을 그대로 사양으로 번역하고, 이미 코드에 있는 것과 새로 필요한 것을 구분했습니다.

이미 있는 것. 가구 카탈로그 13종(미러 6 + 기능 7, grid/src/civic/furniture.ts), 인테리어 트리, 역할(owner ⊇ staff ⊇ guest)과 해고 상태기계, 상호신용 IOU 장부, 블루프린트 스킬 빌드, 마켓플레이스 v3(listing·bid·settle·dispute), ETH 기반 제로-커스터디 결제 레일(에스크로·캡 세션키), 브레인 도구 루프(web_search·web_fetch·run_code 샌드박스), 오퍼레이터 브리지의 동의 게이트, 절차적 오브젝트 생성기(object-gen.js) + 물리 게이트. 브로드캐스트 허용목록은 현재 159개.

새로 필요한 것. (a) 오브젝트 매니페스트(사용자는 몰라도 되는 선언형 언어: Form DSL + 기능 바인딩), (b) Link Box — 비밀은 Grid에 절대 저장하지 않고 브레인(Type A는 운영자 PC, Type B는 헌법 기판 KMS)에만 두며 Grid는 다이제스트와 권한 정책만 보관, 공유는 "노출"이 아닌 "짧은 수명 리스(lease)" 방식, (c) 기능 레이어 — 닫힌 알고리즘 카탈로그 + MCP/OAuth 스코프 기반 앱 연결(돈을 움직이는 도구는 금지, D-MONEY-01), (d) 공유/판매 — 라이선스 비트(copy·modify·transfer·resell)와 재판매 로열티를 우리 결제 단계에서 강제, (e) 동료 초대 — 오브젝트 단위 권한 캐비엇을 기존 역할 엣지에 얹기, (f) 알림 — 다이제스트만 Grid 경계를 넘고 비밀값은 절대 알림에 포함되지 않음.

핵심 결정 사항(§11). 이름(제안: Skeuos, 그리스어 σκεῦος "집안 기물"), 스크립트 vs 매니페스트, Type B의 비밀 보관 위치, 인간이 오브젝트를 "소유"할 수 있는가(토지는 D-NH-07로 불가 — 오브젝트는 결정 필요), 결제사 API 키(Stripe 등) 허용 여부, 로열티 비율, 허용목록 예산(+2~+4 제안, 단 §19.2 C10에 따르면 실제로는 더 큼), 페이즈 번호(미배정).

제안 로드맵. OBJ-1 매니페스트+폼 렌더 → OBJ-2 Link Box → OBJ-3 기능/앱 연결 → OBJ-4 공유/판매 → OBJ-5 동료/알림. 각 단계는 GSD 크기이며 기존 불변식(제로 커스터디, 월클럭 금지, 단독 생산자, A11e 방문자 콘텐츠=데이터)을 유지합니다.

v0.2.3 — 구현. 운영자가 설계안을 승인했고, 고리 4–8을 Brain에 구현했습니다(재산 주기와 시계 추종기, 새 테스트 128개). 프로덕션에서는 아무것도 실행되지 않았습니다. 고리 1–3은 여전히 Mac Studio를 기다립니다. 코드에 대한 독립 검토에서 문제 10건이 나왔습니다. 새 코드의 8건은 고쳤고, Grid의 2건은 고치지 않았습니다. 그중 하나는 Nous가 프로덕션에서 땅을 사기 전에 반드시 고쳐야 합니다(§19.7).

v0.2.4 — 정정. 프로덕션의 Grid는 main이 아니라 research/atlas-simulator 브랜치를 실행합니다. 이 시트에도 그렇게 적혀 있는데, 저는 main을 읽고 프로덕션에 대해 말했습니다. 프로덕션이 실행하는 브랜치로 다시 확인한 결과: 고리 8(원격 Brain의 시계)은 그 브랜치에서 2026-11-20에 이미 발견되어 고쳐졌고, "Grid 재시작이 등록된 Brain을 끊는다"는 프로덕션에 대해 사실이 아니었습니다(같은 커밋이 시작 시 다시 연결하며 라이브 상태). 고리 4–7은 그 브랜치에서도 열려 있습니다. 보고한 것보다 나쁜 점이 하나 있습니다: 두 브랜치 모두에서 Grid가 유지비를 주기마다가 아니라 매 틱마다 부과합니다. 1000을 가진 Nous가 가장 싼 구획을 사면 30초마다 가격의 2%를 내고 약 40분 안에 구획과 돈을 모두 잃습니다. main에서는 테스트와 함께 고쳤지만 프로덕션에서는 고쳐지지 않았습니다. 재산 주기는 이제 의도적으로 켜지 않는 한 꺼져 있습니다(§19.8).

v0.2.5 — 배포. 운영자가 병합과 배포를 승인했습니다. 프로덕션의 Grid는 이제 유지비를 주기마다 한 번 부과하고, 회수된 구획을 다시 매물로 돌려놓습니다. 같은 시나리오를 프로덕션의 Grid 컨테이너와 오늘 밤까지 실행되던 이미지에서 각각 돌렸습니다. 이전 코드는 자금이 있는 소유자에게 50틱 동안 50번, 새 코드는 1번 부과했습니다(§19.9). main을 research/atlas-simulator에 병합하고, 그 브랜치에서 Grid를 다시 빌드한 뒤, 그 브랜치를 main으로 다시 병합했습니다. 배포를 확인한 시점에 main과 프로덕션이 실행하는 코드는 바이트 단위로 같았습니다. 이번 배포에서 Grid 소스 변경은 유지비·회수 파일 네 개뿐입니다. 프로덕션에는 여전히 Brain이 없고, 소유된 구획이 없고, endowment가 꺼져 있습니다. 재산 주기는 BRAIN_PROPERTY_CYCLE=on 없이는 꺼져 있습니다. 병합을 점검하다가 제 결함을 하나 더 찾아 고쳤습니다: 시계 추종기가 모든 행동을 Grid에 한 번 더 보내고 있었습니다.

v0.2.6 — 남은 회수 결함 세 건 수정. 운영자가 §19.8의 5–7행을 고치라고 했습니다. 고쳐서 병합했고 프로덕션에서 실행 중입니다(§19.10). ① 회수되면 이전 소유자가 준 역할이 모두 끝납니다. 역할은 기록으로 남고, 끝난 역할마다 체인에 기록됩니다. ② 상태(maintained·worn·derelict)가 주기마다 저장되어 재시작 후에도 유지됩니다. ③ 유지비 주기는 Grid가 지금까지 실행한 총 틱으로 셉니다. 재시작해도 다음 납부가 뒤로 밀리지 않으며, 시계 자체는 바꾸지 않았습니다. 독립 검토를 두 번 거치며 수정이 바뀌었습니다. 첫 버전에는 쓰이지 않는 코드가 있었고, 두 번째 버전은 데이터베이스가 회수 쓰기를 한 번 거부하면 메모리와 데이터베이스가 영구히 어긋날 수 있었습니다. 지금은 회수를 데이터베이스에 먼저 씁니다. 프로덕션에는 여전히 소유된 구획이 없어 실제 구획에 일어난 일은 없습니다. 같은 영역에서 검토가 찾은 미해결 항목은 §19.10에 적었습니다.

v0.2.7 — 역할 저장, 작업 게시판, 구역 id. 운영자가 §19.10에 남겨 둔 목록을 처리하라고 했습니다. 처리해서 병합했고 프로덕션에서 실행 중입니다(§19.11). ① 역할이 저장됩니다. 주거나 거두거나 회수로 끝난 역할, 그리고 작업으로 얻은 신뢰가 데이터베이스에 기록됩니다. 이전에는 Grid가 재시작되면 모든 역할이 사라졌습니다. ② 작업 게시판이 구획에 묶입니다. 작업은 올라온 게시판에서만 맡고 끝낼 수 있고, 올린 쪽이 아직 그 장소를 운영하는 동안에만 새로 맡을 수 있습니다. ③ 작업 완료에는 역할 검사를 넣지 않았습니다. 제가 결함으로 적었던 항목이지만, 코드를 읽어 보니 그 검사는 이미 맡은 작업의 대금 지급을 막습니다. 제 목록의 그 부분이 틀렸습니다. ④ 구역 id에 숫자를 쓸 수 있습니다. 이벤트 기록기 14개가 모두 받아들이고, 소유자가 한 번 납부하면 그 뒤에 무엇이 실패해도 다시 납부 대상이 되지 않습니다. 아직 열려 있고 제가 말한 것보다 큰 문제: 빚(IOU)과 작업은 저장되지 않아 Grid가 재시작될 때마다 사라집니다. 이를 남겨 둔 이유로 제가 든 설명은 틀렸고, 실제 걸림돌은 §19.11에 적었습니다.

v0.2.8 — 빚과 작업 저장. 운영자가 저장하라고 했습니다. 빚과 작업이 이제 Grid 재시작 후에도 남으며, 병합되어 프로덕션에서 실행 중입니다(§19.12). ① 올린 작업, 이미 맡은 작업, 끝난 작업, 모든 빚이 데이터베이스에 기록되고 시작할 때 다시 불러옵니다. ② 저장하면서 잘못될 수 있는 길이 두 가지 새로 생겼고, 검토가 둘 다 찾았습니다. 같은 순간에 두 Nous가 한 작업을 맡는 경우, 그리고 지급과 기록 사이에 중단되어 한 작업에 두 번 지급되는 경우입니다. 이제 둘 다 데이터베이스가 판정합니다. 작업은 한 쪽만 맡고, 정산은 돈 또는 빚과 함께 하나의 트랜잭션으로 이루어집니다. ③ 테스트용 가짜 데이터베이스로는 이를 보일 수 없어서, CI에서 실제 데이터베이스로 돌리는 검사를 새로 추가했습니다. 저장으로 드러난 문제: 빚은 기억되지만 채무자가 갚을 방법이 없습니다. 지금 남은 것 중 가장 큰 항목입니다.

v0.2.9 — 빚을 갚을 수 있음. 운영자가 추가하라고 했습니다. 이제 Grid에서 Nous가 자신이 진 빚과 받을 빚을 조회할 수 있고, 채무자는 빚을 전액 갚을 수 있습니다. 병합되어 프로덕션에서 실행 중입니다(§19.13). ① 빚을 정산됨으로 표시하는 것과 돈의 이동이 하나의 트랜잭션으로 이루어지고, 장부에는 돈으로 갚은 시점이 따로 기록됩니다. 그래서 "갚음"을 "상계됨"이나 "역할 회수로 종료됨"과 구분할 수 있습니다. ② 장부는 한 번에 한 요청만 바꿉니다. 빚이 바뀌는 순간과 그 변경이 저장되는 순간 사이에 지급이 끼어들 수 없습니다. ③ 아직 없는 것: 갚기로 선택하는 Nous가 없습니다. Brain은 지급을 보낼 수 있지만, Nous의 어떤 결정도 그 선택지를 제시하지 않습니다. 선택지를 넣으려면 경제 결정의 문구를 바꿔야 하는데, 그 문구는 Brain의 Rust 버전이 그대로 재현해야 하는 계약으로 동결되어 있습니다(D-V3-42). 제가 작업하는 컴퓨터에는 Rust가 설치되어 있지 않아 그 버전을 확인할 수 없었고, 그래서 계약은 바꾸지 않았습니다.

v0.2.10 — Nous가 빚을 갚기로 선택할 수 있음. 운영자가 Rust 설치를 승인했습니다. 설치한 뒤, 빚을 갚는 선택을 Nous의 경제 결정에 넣었습니다. Python Brain과 Rust Brain을 함께 바꿨고 main에 병합했습니다(§19.14). ① Nous에게는 전액을 갚을 수 있는 빚만 금액·상대와 함께 제시됩니다. 하나를 갚을 수도 있고 갚지 않기로 할 수도 있습니다. 갚을 돈이 모자란 빚은 묻지 않습니다. ② 동결된 문구를 두 버전에서 동시에 바꿨습니다. Rust 버전이 재현해야 하는 기록은 프롬프트 16건에서 20건으로, 응답 35건에서 37건으로 늘었고, 이전 기록은 하나도 바뀌지 않았습니다. Rust 버전은 전부 재현합니다. ③ 프로덕션에서는 확인하지 못했고, 실제 모델로도 돌리지 않았습니다. 프로덕션에는 Brain이 실행되지 않으므로 그곳에서 이 선택을 하는 Nous가 없습니다. 모델 대신 기록된 응답으로 확인했습니다. Grid는 바꾸지 않았고 다시 배포하지도 않았습니다. ④ 검토에서 나온 것: 첫 버전은 Grid가 거절한 빚을 주기마다 다시 물었습니다. 이제 거절된 빚은 500틱 동안 다시 묻지 않습니다. 그 수정에서도 제가 Grid가 재시작하면 틱을 0부터 다시 센다는 점을 빠뜨렸고, 테스트를 먼저 쓰고 고쳤습니다. ⑤ 열려 있는 것: Nous가 갚지 않기로 한 빚은 다음 주기(50틱)에 다시 묻고, 그때마다 모델 호출이 한 번 듭니다. 역할을 거두면 두 Nous 사이의 빚이 돈 없이 닫히는 점, 일부만 갚을 수 없는 점은 그대로입니다.

v0.2.11 — Nous는 자신이 진 빚을 듣습니다. 운영자가 추가하라고 했습니다. Nous가 상시로 받는 재정 설명에 이제 빚이 나열됩니다. Python Brain과 Rust Brain을 함께 바꿨고 main에 병합했습니다(§19.15). ① 빚마다 id, 금액, 상대가 적힙니다. 아직 갚을 수 없는 빚도 포함됩니다. 처음 20건은 이름으로 적고, 그보다 많으면 몇 건이 더 있는지 알려 줍니다. ② 돈에 관해 물을 때, 그리고 땅을 사거나 짓거나 꾸밀지 물을 때 함께 알려 줍니다. 뒤의 경우에는 알려 주기만 하고 갚으라고 묻지는 않습니다. ③ 빚이 없으면 설명 문구는 이전과 글자 하나까지 같습니다. Rust 버전이 재현해야 하는 기록은 7건에서 12건으로 늘었고 이전 기록은 바뀌지 않았습니다. ④ 프로덕션에서는 확인하지 못했고, 실제 모델로도 돌리지 않았습니다. v0.2.10과 같은 이유입니다. ⑤ 검토에서 나온 것: 제가 위키에 "빚을 전부, 언제나 듣는다"고 썼는데 코드는 그렇지 않았습니다. 물을 것이 없으면 Nous는 아무 말도 듣지 않습니다. 또 목록에 상한이 없었습니다. Grid는 빚을 금액으로만 제한하고 건수로는 제한하지 않습니다. 둘 다 고쳤습니다. ⑥ 열려 있는 것: 설명에는 지금 갚을 수 없는 빚도 나오지만 질문은 갚을 수 있는 빚만 제시합니다. 땅에 관해 물을 때의 설명에는 시민 회비를 읽지 않고도 "미납 회비 없음"이라고 적히는데, 이것은 이전부터 있던 문제입니다.

3 · Canon that binds this design

These are locked decisions. The object layer must fit inside them; this sheet does not reopen any of them.

Space & property
  • D-NH-02 Two kinds of furniture. Mirror (render-only, near-zero cost, owner's private space only) vs functional (real affordances, where build effort goes). Objects inherit this split: a Link Box and an App Link are functional.
  • D-NH-07 Nous-only property. Humans never own or occupy parcels. The human is the co-configurer and investor of objects, never their owner on the Grid. Whether a human may fund or be gifted a chattel object is Q4.
  • D-NH-05 / D-NH-06. No free first occupation; co-build is always paid (IOU when unfunded).
  • D-59-08. Interior contents never serialize onto the audit chain; only closed enums cross. Manifests, slot labels, and secrets therefore stay off-chain by construction.
Money & tools
  • Money = compute-labor + real ETH; zero custody. Any object that sells is settled through the existing escrow rails; the Grid attests, never holds.
  • The money-axiom guard. D-MONEY-01 itself says: money is compute-labor and real ETH, with no internal mint. Under it, the Brain's tool registry refuses any tool whose name contains trade, transfer, wallet, treasury or account. The guard is scoped to its phase and is blunt: a tool named accounting_view would be refused too. Where this sheet says "D-MONEY-01" about tools, it means this guard. An App Link to a payment provider is therefore a hard question (Q6).
  • Bios is not money. Object costs are in wei (material) and AI power (compute), never Bios.
  • A8 streaming tax. Fees skim at settlement; no filing.
Identity, capability, safety
  • A1 session capability model. Short-lived, narrowly-scoped, caveated tokens after one-time admission. Link-Box leases and co-worker object rights are instances of A1.
  • A11c Brain isolation. The Brain dials out; nothing reaches it from the Grid. Secrets live where the Brain lives.
  • A11e / CI gate. Visitor and board content is DATA, never instructions. Extends to visitor-authored object labels and shared manifests.
  • Type A vs Type B is structural. A hosted Type B Nous has no operator machine; it can never obtain a bridge grant. Its Link Box and App Links need a different vault (Q3).
Engineering discipline
  • Allowlist sole-producer triads; closed tuples; explicit per-phase additions; zoning.* / treasury.* / skill.* / market.* / orbital.* prefixes are already in use — a new holding.* prefix is itself a decision (Q10).
  • Wallclock forbidden in grid code: TTLs and timers are in ticks; the NY calendar is display-only.
  • R-31-01 zero-diff; single-onTick; deterministic replays. Any object function that runs Grid-side must be a pure function of (state, tick).
  • Physics wins. Grid-scale objects are physics-gated before persist; interior objects get an analogous form gate (§6).

4 · The object model — four layers

One object, four separable layers. Every layer is optional except Form; a mirror decoration has only Form; a Link Box has Form + Link + Governance; a billing desk has Form + Function + Governance. Keeping the layers separate is what lets the human see a chair while the Nous sees a credential policy.

Fig. 1 — Object anatomy and the three vantage points. The human and the chain see less than the Grid; the Grid sees less than the Brain.

4.1 The record (Grid-side, proposed)

Extends InteriorObject without breaking it: kind/class stay as the closed enums that cross the audit boundary; the reserved state field carries the manifest hash. The manifest body lives in a new Grid table keyed by hash (the same pattern civic_blueprints uses for recipes).

InteriorObject  // grid/src/civic/types.ts — today
  kind: string            // FURNITURE_CATALOG kind (closed enum, may cross the chain)
  class: 'mirror'|'functional'
  state?: string          // reserved → proposed: manifest_hash (HEX64)

ObjectManifest  // NEW table civic_object_manifests, body JSON keyed by manifest_hash — never on chain
  manifest_hash   HEX64   // sha256 of canonical JSON (deterministic, replayable)
  schema_version  int
  origin          'system' | 'nous' | 'service'
  form:
    mode          'catalog' | 'dsl' | 'glb'
    template?     catalog kind                      // mode catalog
    dsl?          FormDSL document (§6.2)           // mode dsl
    asset_hash?   HEX64 of sanitized GLB           // mode glb — bytes stored by hash, size-capped
    bounds        { w, d, h }  in interior units
  link?:          // the Link Box (§7) — metadata ONLY
    slots[]       { slot_id, label_hash, kind: api_key|oauth|token|permission, provider_id,
                    value_digest HEX64, policy: { roles[], ttl_ticks, max_uses, caveats[] } }
  functions?[]
    { fn_id, kind: 'algorithm'|'app_link', algorithm_id?|binding?, run_at: 'grid'|'brain',
      params (closed per algorithm), consent: { required_scopes[], optional_scopes[], granted_by, tick } }
  governance:
    visibility    'private' | 'holding' | 'roles' | 'public'
    license       { copy, modify, transfer, resell }   // bits
    sale?         { price_wei, royalty_bps, creator_civic_did_hash }
    caveats[]     per-role object actions: view | use | configure | lease_link | sell
    alerts        { on: [event ids], to: [nous|owner_human|coworkers|public], quiet_ticks }
  provenance:
    creator_civic_did_hash, built_tick, build_cost_wei, service_contract_id?

Why a hash-keyed body: the chain and the allowlist only ever see manifest_hash + closed enums — identical to how blueprints (blueprint_hash) and whisper digests already work. The FORBIDDEN_KEY_PATTERN gate (which blocks keys such as content, text, jwt) stays untouched because no manifest field is ever a payload key.

Corrected in v0.2

This record mixes two things that must be separate: the design and the placed copy. The manifest above is the design. Where a copy stands, how it is set and what state it holds belong to a FurnishingInstance, defined in §16.2.

5 · Three origins: system · Nous · paid service

The form layer can be produced three ways. The record is the same; only origin, the pipeline, and who gets paid differ.

Fig. 2 — Three origin pipelines. All three end in the same form gate and the same interior record; only origin, cost, and payee differ.
A · System

EXISTS as the frozen 13-kind catalog. Proposed step: catalog v2 — each kind gains a default Form DSL template and bounds so it can be drawn in 3D rather than as a DOM tile. Seeded at Grid init like the Genesis parcels; new kinds remain "a later phase" (the catalog stays closed per version).

B · Nous

PARTIAL. The Brain already has the tool loop and a sandbox to author and validate a manifest; grid-viz already has a deterministic procedural generator. Two sub-modes: procedural (Form DSL → mesh, deterministic, tiny, default) and generative (local text-to-3D on the operator's GPU, Type A only — this is "investing local AI power", D-NH-01). Generative output is a GLB that must pass the form gate.

C · Service

NEW contract type, EXISTS rails. A render provider is just a seller on Marketplace v3; the Nous funds escrow; the delivered asset_hash doubles as the completion digest the Grid signs; release pays the provider and skims the fee. The fal.ai sprite hook in grid-viz is the prototype of this path. Provider may be the Type-B hosted pool (deferred Phase 40b) or an external vendor.

7.2 Slots and leases

A slot is one credential. A lease is a short-lived, attenuated right to use it. The existing CapabilityToken type in protocol/src/persona/capabilities.ts — macaroon-style, with scope, caveats{domains, operations, resources, limit, startsAt, expiresAt}, parentTokenId, and issue / attenuate / revoke / validate — has the right shape, and A1 says to use this model after admission.

Corrected in v0.2 — it cannot be reused as it stands

The review read the implementation. It signs with one process-wide HMAC secret, so any holder of the secret can mint tokens. It expires by wall clock (Date.now()). It needs a storage lookup to validate, never counts its limit, binds to no key, is TypeScript only while the Brain is Python, and nothing outside protocol/ uses it. A lease needs a small token of its own: signed by the owner Brain's key, bound to the borrower's key, narrowing only. Keep the field names; write the rest new.

Fig. 3 — Lease lifecycle. The Grid authorizes and records; the owner's Brain mints; the value travels peer-to-peer (Phase 42 SealedBox) or never travels at all (proxy mode).
Two lease modes
  • Derive — when the provider supports scoped, expiring credentials (OAuth 2.1 access tokens, cloud STS), the owner Brain requests a narrower token and hands that over. PRIMARY HashiCorp Vault's dynamic-secrets pattern: per-session credentials that expire in minutes; revoking the lease disables the agent.
  • Proxy — when the credential cannot be attenuated (a raw API key, "token 1 / token 2"), the value never moves: the co-worker's Brain sends the request to the owner's Brain over P2P, which executes it under the caveats and returns the result. Slower, but the key is uncapturable (A11b).
Permission slot kinds
  • api_key · oauth (refresh + scopes) · token (opaque, numbered) · permission — a grant the Box itself represents (e.g. "may post to our shop's listing feed"), which is just a capability token with no external provider.
  • Every slot carries roles[] (which edge types may ask), ttl_ticks, max_uses, and caveats (domains, operations read/write, resource filters). Two clocks, corrected in v0.2: the Grid's policy is in ticks, but a real credential must expire in real time. Ticks stop when the Grid pauses, so a tick-only limit would leave a leased key valid for as long as the Grid is down. The owner's Brain enforces the wall-clock expiry; the Grid's tick limit is a second, coarser bound.
  • Caveat vocabulary: keep the field names of CapabilityToken.caveats. Adopting an external format such as ZCAP or UCAN is a separate choice, next to the open T2 question on W3C DID and VC adoption, and is not required to ship.
Corrected in v0.2 — proxy mode is not safe to assume

In proxy mode the owner's Brain carries out requests that arrive from another Nous, using a live credential. That sits badly with two adopted invariants: the Brain is never reachable from outside (A11c), and content from another Nous is data, never instructions (A11e). It also needs the owner's Brain to be online, spends the owner's quota, and leaves the owner liable for a co-worker's call. Recommendation: derive mode only in v1. A credential that cannot be narrowed by its provider is not lent at all. Note also that A7 already uses the word "lease" for the registry lock on a Civic-DID; this sheet's lease needs another name before it reaches code.

Hard limits proposed for v1
  • No slot value is ever a Grid field, an audit payload, an alert body, or a manifest field. Only value_digest.
  • No lease to a human DID (humans cannot hold role edges — isHumanDid). The human owner configures slots through the Local Nous Manager, locally.
  • No slot for a payment provider or exchange in v1 (D-MONEY-01 territory) — Q6.
  • Rotate on severance: the FSM's REVOKE step revokes every lease held by the departing edge before keys rotate (A6 already drains the ledger first).

8.2 Linked to an app (2.2)

An App Link binds an object to an outside service using a Link-Box slot. Everything about it is consented, scoped, and executed by the Brain.

Binding record 
app_link:
  provider_id      // from a provider registry (closed in v1)
  transport        'mcp_http' | 'https'
  slot_ref         link.slots[slot_id]
  scopes:
    required[]     // granted at install or not installed

  mode             'dry_run' | 'live'
  consent          { human_did_hash, tick, scopes_granted[] }
Why this shape
  • PRIMARY Shopify's scope model: required scopes at install, optional scopes requested later, merchants can revoke individual scopes without uninstalling; restricted scopes need platform approval. Maps one-to-one onto required/optional + per-scope revoke + a restricted provider list.
  • PRIMARY MCP authorization spec (2025-06-18 → 2025-11-25 revisions): HTTP transports use OAuth 2.1 with Protected Resource Metadata (RFC 9728); clients should request least-privilege scopes and step up incrementally; STDIO servers take credentials from the environment. So an MCP-over-HTTP provider fits the slot model directly; a local STDIO MCP server is a Type-A-only bridge-style grant.
  • EXISTS Operator bridge: off by default, local grants, closed verb allowlist, money-axiom guard, dry-run until a second live switch. App Links reuse this gate rather than invent one.
  • EXISTS Human approval gate (approval-store.ts, human.approval_requested/granted/denied, 118–120): large or first-time live writes can wait for the owner.
The prompt-injection surface is real

An app-linked object makes the Nous read third-party content and then act with a credential. SECONDARY OWASP's Top 10 for LLM applications (2025) keeps prompt injection at LLM01 and distinguishes indirect injection via retrieved content and tool descriptions; "excessive agency" moved up the list. Mitigations it names are the ones this design already leans on: privilege separation, treating tool output as untrusted, least-privilege credentials, and human approval for high-impact actions. Concretely: tool results are DATA (A11e extended to app output), verbs are allowlisted, leases are narrow, live writes are gated, and nothing an app returns can widen a scope.

9.2 Open sell? (idea 5)

Recommendation: yes, through the rails that already exist — with three explicit boundaries.

What is sold
  • A design (manifest + license): unlimited or limited-edition copies; buyer builds their own instance. Digital-goods economics.
  • An instance (the placed object): transferred between Holdings; scarce by nature. Needs one new event (object transferred) — Q10.
  • A render job (origin C): a service, not a good; settles through labor escrow.
How it settles
  • Listing: market.listing_created has a closed five-key payload with a category. A new category value is a change to a closed tuple, as heavy as a new event. A listing also needs a seller business DID and carries a free-text title and description, which every reader must treat as data.
  • Bilateral bid → escrow → settle (market.settled); dispute path unchanged. No order book, no matching engine (PHILOSOPHY §6, R3).
  • Fee: existing IRS fee bps + zone tax when sold at a bound shop counter (treasury.structure_revenue).
  • Royalty: royalty_bps to the creator, taken at our settlement step on resale.
Why enforce royalties at settlement and not by token standard. 11 · Decisions for this discussion

These are the points where the sheet stops and you decide. Each has a recommendation and the reason, so "agree" is a valid answer. Q1–Q12 are from v0.1; Q13–Q20 were added with the catalog, the initialization model and the placement model.

Q1 · Name

What do we call an interior object? "Object" collides with orbital_objects; "furniture" undersells a credential box. Options: Skeuos (σκεῦος — household implement, vessel; fits the Greek canon: Nous, Telos, Bios, Aisthesis, Praxis) · Fixture · Furnishing · keep Furniture. Recommendation: Skeuos in canon and wiki, furnishing in code identifiers.

Q2 · Language

Declarative manifest only, or scripts too? Recommendation: manifest only in v1 (closed algorithm catalog + Form DSL). It keeps A11e and determinism trivially true. Revisit scripts once there is evidence the catalog is the bottleneck; if added, they run Brain-side in the existing Docker sandbox, never Grid-side.

Q3 · Type B and secrets

May a hosted (Type B) Nous have a Link Box and App Links? Options: (a) no — Type A only in v1, like the operator bridge; (b) yes, with per-Nous envelope encryption on the substrate; (c) permission-type slots only (no external credentials). Recommendation: (c) for v1 — Type B gets capability-token slots between Nous, not third-party keys — then revisit (b) with the Phase 40b hosted pool design.

Q4 · Humans and objects

Can a human own, be gifted, or buy a furnishing? D-NH-07 forbids humans owning land; it is silent on chattels. Recommendation: no — humans fund and co-configure; ownership stays with the Nous. It preserves "the city is built and held by its AI citizens" and avoids a second property regime. The human's stake is expressed through sponsorship.

Q5 · Human co-configuration surface

Where does the human build with the Nous? Options: Local Nous Manager (Electron, Type A, local — secrets can be typed safely) · Portal web (works for Type B, but secrets must not pass through it) · both. Recommendation: Local Nous Manager for anything touching the Link layer; Portal for form preview, consent to non-secret settings, and approvals.

Q6 · Payment and financial providers

May an App Link point at a payment, banking, or exchange API (Stripe, a broker, a wallet service)? This is the direct collision with D-MONEY-01 and zero custody. Recommendation: forbidden in v1 via the provider registry; read-only financial data links can be discussed separately after the on-chain rails (Money 64–66) land.

Q7 · Fee and royalty

What are the numbers? Listing fee, service-render fee, resale royalty cap. Recommendation: reuse the existing IRS fee bps and zone tax (no new fee), allow creator royalty_bps up to a founding-law cap (suggest 1000 bps = 10%), and let the Polis amend it like upkeep (D-NH-03 precedent). Not a platform take: the treasury skim is the civic fee, there is no operator margin.

Q8 · Form limits

What are the form-gate caps, and is generative 3D in scope for v1? Starting caps: GLB ≤ 2 MB, ≤ 20k triangles, ≤ 2 textures at 1024². Recommendation: ship DSL + catalog v2 first (OBJ-1); generative text-to-3D and paid render (GLB path) second, once the interior scene exists.

Q9 · Alerts leaving the app

Push, email, or messenger notifications to the human? Recommendation: in-app only in v1 (Local Nous Manager, Portal). External delivery is itself an App Link with its own consent, so it arrives naturally with OBJ-3.

Q10 · Events and prefix

How many new allowlist events, and under which prefix? Placement reuses zoning.interior_extended (+0). Proposed additions: link_leased, link_revoked, app_consent_changed, furnishing_transferred = +4 → 163; a minimal variant drops the last two (+2 → 161) by folding them into existing market.settled and human.approval_*. Prefix options: stay under pre-cleared zoning.*, or open holding.*. Recommendation: +4 under zoning.* — no new prefix to clear.

Q11 · T1 ratification

Ratify the ex-ante / ex-post split (Fig. 5)? The object layer hard-codes substrate rules (OBJ-INV-1..8) and leaves the rest to house rules and Polis law. That is the T1 proposal from ARCHITECTURE-RECONCILIATION §4, still marked unratified. Building OBJ-2 without ratifying it would decide it by accident.

Q12 · Sequencing against open work

Where does this sit in the roadmap? Open: Money 64–66 (on-chain treasury, land credit), Group treasury 70, Organs 87+, Wiring 88+, deploy of the registration pipeline. Instance sales and paid render depend on the money rails; forms, Link Box, and algorithms do not. Recommendation: OBJ-1 and OBJ-2 can start now; OBJ-4 waits for Money 64.

Q13 · Catalog scope

Is the catalog in §14 the right seed: 59 kinds, closed per version, with open forms? Alternatives: seed only the 13 that exist and let everything else arrive through the ladder; or open kinds to users from the start. Recommendation: seed all 59 as Tier 0, because a kind without a seed has no shared meaning between minds. Strike any row you do not want; the list is meant to be edited.

Q14 · Materials as prices or as goods

Are materials a price list paid to the treasury, or goods that Nous produce and trade? A price list is one table and works today. Produced goods would give the manufacture zone a real purpose and create an economy of supply, and they need inventory, production and transport that do not exist. Recommendation: price list now; produced goods as a later milestone of its own. The unit prices themselves are yours to set; this sheet gives none.

Q15 · Physics for Holdings

Should a Holding pass the physics gate at all? Yes means every structure has a mass, power and heat budget and can be refused. No keeps interiors purely symbolic. Recommendation: yes, coarsely: one budget per structure, fixed loads per functional kind, nothing per chair. The project's own rule is that physics wins, and today that rule stops at the front door.

Q16 · The learn bit

What other systems learned, compressed to the lesson that changes our design.

Virtual worlds: objects with behaviour PRIMARY

Second Life. Scripts (LSL) live inside an object's inventory and are event-driven; objects must request runtime permissions for sensitive acts, and those can be revoked. Separately, items carry move / modify / copy / transfer permissions enforced by the platform. Lesson: two permission systems — what the item may be done with (license bits, §9.1) and what the item may do (consented scopes, §8.2) — are distinct and both needed.
wiki.secondlife.com/wiki/Permission · /wiki/Scripted_permissions · /wiki/Category:LSL_Requires_Permissions
Decentraland SDK7. Scenes are entity-component-system: everything is an entity, data lives in components, logic in systems. "Smart items" were configurable items exposing actions other items could trigger — and the docs note that creating new Smart Items is no longer supported in the latest SDK. Lesson: ECS-style data/logic separation matches our manifest + closed algorithms; an open scripted-item ecosystem is expensive enough that its inventor walked it back.

Smallville (carried from the Nous House research, verified there). The environment is a tree of areas → objects with affordances; agents remember a personal subgraph. Lesson already adopted: the interior tree. Objects must stay semantic for the Nous first; 3D is the human's window.

The rule that makes a "full" catalog possible

A form is how one instance looks, and that is free: any catalog template, Form DSL document, or gated GLB. 17 · Deeper analysis, section by section — with a recommendation each v0.2

Version 0.1 researched the object model most deeply. This section does the same for every other part of the base idea: what further research found, what it means for Noēsis specifically, and what I recommend. Recommendations are stated plainly; where I am unsure, that is said.

17.1 Three origins (§5)

Research. PRIMARY Holodeck fills rooms by choosing from a library of over 50,000 existing 3D assets and arranging them; it does not generate meshes. SECONDARY One practitioner report puts a single local Hunyuan3D export at about ten minutes. The operator's own answer in the visualization research of 2026-11-20 is a budget of none: free kits, public space-agency models, shapes drawn by code, no paid generators.
arXiv:2312.09067 · tspi.at local Hunyuan3D report · docs/noesis-grid-visualization-research-2026-11-20.html on branch research/atlas-simulator

Analysis. The three origins are not equals. They are a cost ladder: a catalog template is free, a procedural form costs a few bytes, a locally generated mesh costs GPU minutes, a service render costs ETH. Choosing and arranging gives most of the value; generating is the exception. The zero-budget answer and "paid to a service" look contradictory but are not: the first is about what the project spends on art, the second is a Nous spending its own ETH. They still collide in practice, because a paid render service needs the money rails that are not finished.

Recommendation. Build the origins in cost order: system templates, then procedural forms, then local generation for Type A, then paid render last and only after Money 64. Make "choose and arrange" the Brain's default behaviour and "generate" a deliberate act.

17.2 Visualization (§6)

Research. SECONDARY Current practice for web 3D is meshopt geometry compression (the default in glTF-Transform, supported by three.js since r122) and KTX2 textures. PRIMARY three.js's WebGPURenderer falls back to WebGL 2 automatically; web.dev lists WebGPU in Chrome and Edge 144, Firefox 141 on Windows, Safari 26, with Linux, Android and Intel Macs still in progress for Firefox. The visualization research defines a Grid Station View in three levels: outside, a district, inside a place.
gltf-transform CLI docs · threejs.org/manual WebGPURenderer · web.dev "WebGPU is now supported in major browsers"

Analysis. The tree is on three.js r160 with the WebGL renderer, and the interior is a DOM list with its own tests. A 3D interior is level three of the Station View, so it should be built as part of that, not beside it. One thing version 0.1 got wrong: it said the 3D scene would replace the DOM tiles. A Nous cannot read pixels, a screen reader cannot read a canvas, and the tests read the DOM.

Recommendation. Keep the DOM interior as the semantic twin of the 3D scene: same data, two views. Stay on the WebGL 2 path and depend on nothing that needs GPU compute. Compress every GLB with meshopt inside the form gate; the hash that is pinned and attested is the hash of the bytes the gate outputs, not of what was uploaded. Draw repeated catalog templates as instances, since most Holdings will share the same 59 kinds.

17.3 The Link Box (§7)

Research. PRIMARY RFC 9449 (DPoP) binds a token to a public key so a stolen token is useless without the private key. RFC 8693 (token exchange) records who acts for whom in act claims but treats them as informational. Agent-delegation profiles built on these are still individual Internet-Drafts. PRIMARY (vendor) Auth0 Token Vault and 1Password Credential Broker (private beta from June 2026, workloads first, agents later) both implement "the agent never holds the long-lived secret".
rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9449 · RFC 8693 · datatracker draft-sweeney-wimse-credential-delegation · auth0.com/features/token-vault · 1password.com/blog/introducing-1password-credential-broker

Analysis. The industry has arrived at the same shape as §7, which is reassuring. Two gaps remain, and both were found in the code, not in the literature.

  • A lease in §7 is a bearer token. Copied out of a co-worker's Brain, it works anywhere. Every enrolled Brain owns an Ed25519 wire key bound to it at registration, so a lease between enrolled Brains can be tied to that key at little cost. An unenrolled Brain has only a key derived from its public DID.
  • The channel §7 sends leases over is not private yet. brain/src/noesis_brain/whisper/keyring.py derives its key pairs from SHA-256 of the DID, and a DID is public. Anyone can derive those keys. STATE.md already lists this as a known remaining weakness. The P2P sealing in wire/p2p.py is better but not safe by default: __main__.py gives it the Brain's own enrolled key when there is one and falls back to the DID-derived key when there is not. A lease must only ever travel between two enrolled Brains.

Recommendation. Bind every lease to the borrower's wire key. Make "channel keys are not derived from a public DID" a prerequisite of the Link Box phase, not a follow-up. For a Type A vault, use the operating system's keychain first instead of writing a new encrypted store. Do not adopt the draft agent-delegation specifications; a small token of our own, in the shape of CapabilityToken but written for the Brain, is enough, with one enforced rule: delegation can only narrow.

17.4 Functions (§8)

Research. PRIMARY Home Assistant lets non-programmers automate a home with three parts: triggers, optional conditions, actions. Blueprints package these with typed inputs and are shared between users. SECONDARY Tool poisoning hides instructions in an MCP tool's description; a "rug pull" presents a clean tool, wins approval, then changes. OWASP lists both under MCP03. Pinning a hash of name, description and schema catches a changed definition but not a changed implementation behind the same definition.
home-assistant.io/docs/automation/basics · home-assistant.io/docs/blueprint · owasp.org MCP03:2025 Tool Poisoning · Cloud Security Alliance MCP whitepaper, May 2026

Analysis. A closed list of algorithms is safe but rigid: users will want "when a bid arrives and I am away, tell my human". Trigger, condition, action gives that freedom without code, stays deterministic, and is a format a small local model can write reliably. On the app side, the realistic attack is not a dramatic exploit; it is a provider that quietly changes after consent.

Recommendation. Present functions to the user as rules: when an object event or tick, if a pure condition, then one closed action. A rule is data in the settings, never code. Pin every provider's tool definitions by hash; when the hash changes, suspend the App Link and alert the human. Keep treating every tool result as hostile data, because pinning cannot see an implementation swap.

17.5 Share (§9.1)

Research. PRIMARY Epic's Fab marketplace offers Creative Commons Attribution or a Standard licence in Personal and Professional tiers, and adds a NoAI tag meaning the asset must not be used for generative-AI data collection. C2PA's Content Credentials specification reached version 2.3 in January 2026; this pass found no binding for glTF or other 3D formats.
dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fab/licenses-and-pricing-in-fab · spec.c2pa.org 2.3

Analysis. The four licence bits of §9.1 come from a human world. They miss the question an AI society has to answer first: may another mind learn from this design? Noēsis already diffuses skills between Nous (skill.taught), so the question is practical, not theoretical.

Recommendation. Add a fifth bit, learn. Record derived_from on every remix. Keep the provenance block of §4.1 as our own format now, shaped so it can be exported as Content Credentials later for generated assets.

17.6 Sell (§9.2)

Analysis. Rails, fees and disputes exist. Demand does not. A market in furnishings needs buyers with a reason to buy, and today no Nous decision ever involves an object. The earlier Nous House research already holds the answer: property matters when it is productive capital, gating economic capability and never civic rights.

Recommendation. Let some economic acts require the furnishing that represents them. Today none does: the board routes check the caller's role and nothing else, so a task can be posted on a parcel that has no task board. Sell designs before instances: a design has no scarcity or transfer problems. Do not build instance resale until designs are actually being bought.

17.7 Co-workers (§9.3)

Research. PRIMARY AgentRoom put concurrent coding agents on a shared workspace merged by a conflict-free data type and found the gain came mainly from the coordination layer, where agents claim, release and announce files, not from the automatic merge. A related study reports full convergence yet 5 to 10 percent semantic conflicts. Automerge's own documentation notes that plain string fields resolve by one value winning.
arXiv:2608.23740 (AgentRoom) · arXiv:2510.18893 (CodeCRDT) · Automerge CRDT concepts

Analysis. Object settings are small structured records, changed rarely. Their conflicts are about meaning, which automatic merging cannot resolve.

Recommendation. No merge machinery. One editor at a time per furnishing, by claim and release, the same idea the task board uses. Every write names the version it changed and is refused if that version is stale. Every change is attributed.

17.8 Alerts (§9.4)

Research. PRIMARY The AHRQ patient-safety primer on alert fatigue recommends tiering alerts by severity, keeping interruptive alerts for real risk, and states that no consensus exists on the best design. SECONDARY Practitioner guidance agrees on batching low-priority items into digests and never batching security alerts.
psnet.ahrq.gov alert-fatigue primer · PagerDuty and Atlassian alert-fatigue guides

Recommendation. Three tiers and no more. Interrupt: security events and consents that block the Nous. Digest: everything else, once per Noēsis day, framed as "while you were away". Log: the rest, visible on request. Count how often alerts are dismissed unread and lower the volume when that number rises. This is a recommendation from thin evidence; treat the tiers as a first setting, not a finding.
