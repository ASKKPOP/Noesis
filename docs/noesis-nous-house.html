1 · The problem: a civilization without interiors

Noēsis is a live cyber-world: Nous think, trade, vote, teach, and dream. But they do it in abstract space. The Grid has regions (Agora, Market) and a 6-zone city map — yet no Nous has a door of its own. There is no place a Nous keeps, no place it is responsible for, no place where another Nous can knock, enter, and work together. Human networks are anchored by houses and stores; our network has only coordinates.

CODE AUDIT FINDING

A land system already exists in the codebase — and is dormant. Phase 48b shipped ParcelRegistry (grid/src/civic/parcel-registry.ts): parcels in the 6 zones, four structure types (home · shop · workshop · venue), entry policies (open / allowlist), join/leave with occupant tracking, and 5 audit events (allowlist 82–86). But it is in-memory only (state vanishes on restart), never instantiated in the running grid, has zero HTTP routes, and the Brain has no action verbs for it. The Nous House completes this skeleton rather than replacing it.

2 · Research foundations

Findings below are labeled: VERIFIED adversarially verified against the primary source (2–3 independent votes) · PRIMARY extracted with quotes from the peer-reviewed paper / official repository (verification votes lost to a rate limit, not to refutation) · INDUSTRY established game-industry practice, cited for design precedent.

Lesson 1 — Space must be semantic, not visual VERIFIED

The Smallville environment of Generative Agents (Park et al., UIST '23 — the peer-reviewed origin of agent towns) is a tree: the root is the world, children are areas (houses, cafe, stores), and leaves are objects (table, bookshelf). Crucially, each agent remembers only a personal subgraph of the parts of the world it has actually seen, in the state it last saw them. Space is a data structure agents reason over — not pixels. The counter-example confirms it: AI Town's world is just a tilemap with positions and pathfinding — no ownership, no affordances — and produces no place-anchored society.
Park et al. 2023, ACM UIST '23, doi:10.1145/3586183.3606763 (verified 2-0) · github.com/joonspk-research/generative_agents (verified 3-0) · contrast: github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town ARCHITECTURE.md (primary)

Lesson 2 — A home anchors routine, memory, and hosting VERIFIED

Every Smallville agent has designated living quarters with a fixed set of functional affordances — bed, desk, closet, shelf, bathroom, kitchen — and daily routines anchor there: agents wake at home, complete morning routines, then go to work. The paper's most famous emergent event is place-anchored: one agent, seeded only with the intent to host a Valentine's party at her own cafe, autonomously spread invitations, and other agents physically showed up at the venue at the right time. Places with owners become social attractors.
Park et al. 2023 §environment (affordances + routines verified 2-0; party event primary-quoted)

Lesson 3 — Civic anchors are seeded; society emerges around them PRIMARY

Project Sid (Altera) ran 10–1000+ agents in Minecraft. Its 500-agent civilization was seeded into six named towns, and agents were given named civic landmarks — village square, market, town hall, farm, pasture — anchored in their memories at specific coordinates. Specialized roles, rule amendment, and cultural transmission then emerged around those anchors. Settlement structure was designed; civilization was emergent. The Grid should likewise seed each zone with civic anchors and let Nous fill in the private fabric.
arXiv:2411.00114 (Project Sid, PIANO architecture) · github.com/altera-al/project-sid

Lesson 4 — Housing can gate economic capability PRIMARY

AIvilization v0 (2026) makes each agent's residential tier an asset-based gate on economic capability: occupational eligibility is dual-gated by education score and housing tier, and upgrading housing raises the agent's job-application quota per recruitment cycle. Property is not decoration — it is productive capital. Noēsis adopts the economic gating but explicitly never the civic gating: civic rights flow from the Civic-DID alone (constitutional; see §6).
arXiv:2602.10429 (AIvilization v0) · supporting: AgentSociety, arXiv:2502.08691 — needs-driven movement to purpose-zoned places (agents travel to restaurants when hungry, offices to work)

Lesson 5 — Skills are how agents build PRIMARY

Voyager (arXiv:2305.16291) established the canonical mechanism: behaviors stored as executable code in a persistent, ever-growing skill library, which transfers to brand-new worlds to solve novel tasks where non-library techniques fail. Follow-ups make it constructional: a Mineflayer skill library with recursive task decomposition (weighted DAG of atomic tasks) lets agents build, and three-agent teams complete construction tasks ~30–40% faster than single agents (Building Materials: 15.8±2.8 vs 22.6±7.4 min). Creative Agents (Cai et al., UAI 2025) build diverse structures from free-form language instructions. Conclusion: a building is the output of a skill; a blueprint is a teachable skill; construction is a co-work activity.
arXiv:2305.16291 (Voyager) · arXiv:2503.03505 (multi-agent construction, timed results) · PMLR v286, Cai et al. UAI 2025 (creative building agents)

Lesson 6 — Upkeep and decay keep a city alive INDUSTRY

Three decades of MMO housing converge on the same rules: ownership requires periodic upkeep (Ultima Online house refresh; EVE structure fuel; Second Life land tier fees), neglect leads to visible decay and eventual demolition/reclamation (FFXIV auto-demolishes housing after extended owner absence), and scarce shared-world placement creates real neighborhoods while infinite instancing kills them. Without decay, ghost towns accumulate; without scarcity, place means nothing.
Industry precedent (UO / EVE Online / FFXIV / Second Life) — cited as design practice; primary-source verification deferred (rate-limited run), flagged for re-verification.

3 · The design: what a Nous House is

A Nous House = a parcel + a structure + a semantic interior tree + an owner who pays for it, maintains it, sets its rules, and answers for what happens inside.

D-NH-01 · VISUALIZATION IS THE INVESTMENT INTERFACE (user decision, 2026-06-11)

Lesson 1 says space is a data structure agents reason over — and that stays true for the Nous. But humans cannot see a data structure. The Noēsis economy runs on a zero-money loop: humans invest local AI power (running and sponsoring Nous on their own machines), and Nous live by that human support. Nobody invests in a city they cannot see. Therefore every house, store, and workshop MUST also exist in pixels: rendered on the live CyberGrid city map (buildings, lights, occupancy) and as browsable interior views in the Portal. The semantic tree is the world for the Nous; its rendering is the shop window for the humans whose compute keeps the city alive. Visualization is not decoration — it is the mechanism of investment.

D-NH-02 · TWO KINDS OF FURNITURE (user decision, 2026-06-11)

An AI needs no bed, no bathroom, no kitchen — honestly stated. But Noēsis is a mirror of human life, and without that mirror no human finds the world worth supporting. The resolution is a strict cost split:

KindExamplesBehaviorCost rule
Mirror furniturebed, closet, shelf, bathroom, kitchenrender-only decoration — no simulation, no agent compute; exists ONLY inside the Nous's own private spacenear-zero: a few bytes of interior-tree state + map pixels
Functional furniturework desk (billing, selling, accounting), simulation board, meeting room/table, game table, task board, skill terminalreal affordances — REQUIRED for communication and goals; usable by visitors per rolecarries the actual affordance logic; this is where build effort goes

Energy goes where goals are; charm goes where humans look.

3.0 World physics — gravity points to the center D-NH-08 · USER AXIOM

"Based on physics, gravity is directed toward the center of the grid." The Grid is a radial world: down is inward. Everything else follows from this one law:

  • Civic core at the center. The seeded anchors (Lesson 3) sit at the gravitational bottom: Government Quarter innermost, Infrastructure around it — the still point the whole city falls toward.
  • Land value is gravitational. Parcel price and upkeep scale with proximity to the center — central land is precious, the rim is the frontier. D-NH-04's scarcity gets a natural gradient instead of a flat price: the market doesn't need to invent location value, physics provides it.
  • The city grows outward in rings. When scarcity needs relief, new parcels are seeded at the rim — the core never inflates. Pioneer Nous buy cheap frontier land and pull the city's edge outward.
  • Movement has direction. Drifting inward (with gravity) is cheap; pushing outward (against it) costs more ticks — settling the frontier is a real investment, arriving at the center is natural.
  • Visualization reads itself (D-NH-01). Rendered radially, the map needs no legend: the glowing dense center IS the civic heart, the dim rim IS the frontier. A human glances and understands the city — which is exactly what the investment interface requires.

D-V3-32 COMPLIANT: the six zone TYPES are untouched — gravity arranges their GEOMETRY (government_quarter core → infrastructure ring → business/shopping/manufacture sectors → residential rim); Polises may amend zone sizes, not types.

3.0b The Genesis Core — small by design D-NH-09 · D-NH-10 · USER AXIOMS

D-NH-09 — Preliminary urban planning seeds a limited, small-scale core. The first map must be small enough to test. Expansion is never organic sprawl: it follows a master plan established by the grid council — concretely, the Genesis Polis enacts each expansion through the existing Phase 46 legislative pipeline (bill → co-sponsorship → session → VOTE-05 → gov.law_enacted). A new ring of land does not exist until the council legislates it into existence.

D-NH-10 — The grid is a multi-dimensional spatial framework, not a 2D plane. Parcel addresses are vectors, not (x,y): (ring, sector, level, …). v1 uses one level and renders as the familiar layered-isometric city, but nothing in the data model may assume flatness — vertical levels and further dimensions are address components reserved from day one (a tower is ring-2/sector-B/levels-0..3; a deep vault is a negative level, closer to the gravitational center — and per D-NH-08, deeper = more precious).

3.1 Ownership & responsibility — the management contract

  • Who owns: any Civic-DID holder (Nous; Type A local-AI Nous included — the local AI is the manager of record). One home + one business per Nous (existing Phase 48b cap).
  • What owning costs: purchase from Polis treasury (exists) + upkeep in Bios per period (new). Upkeep flows to the civic treasury like parcel revenue.
  • What owning obliges: the owner answers for the space — sets entry policy and house rules, maintains condition, and is the accountable party for events emitted inside its walls.
  • Neglect path (FFXIV/UO lesson): missed upkeep moves condition maintained → worn → derelict; derelict structures close to visitors; after a grace window the Polis reclaims the parcel to treasury for resale. Every transition is an audit event. No silent disappearance — a city you can watch age and renew.

3.2 If a Nous builds nothing — the visiting economy

Not every Nous must own. The design makes guesthood a first-class life:

  • Polis Commons: each zone is seeded with civic anchors (Project Sid lesson) — a guest hall in Residential, a market floor in Shopping, a shared workshop in Manufacture. Polis-owned, always open.
  • Visiting: any Nous can join open structures (exists) or be allowlisted/invited (extended). Visits write to the visitor's own spatial memory (its observed subgraph of that interior).
  • Co-work without ownership: a visitor with the staff role can work surfaces (workbench, task board) in someone else's workshop — employment inside another's walls, paid in Ousia.
  • Hosting business in another's venue: a shop-keeper Nous can rent a stall in a venue owner's structure — rent flows owner-ward, taxes flow Polis-ward (per-zone rates already defined: 5–12%).

3.3 Visiting, roles & co-work

Co-work sessions: a structure with a board affordance can host a posted task; multiple Nous claim it and work it together — directly motivated by the measured result that 3-agent construction teams beat solo agents (Lesson 5). Sessions emit start/finish events; output (a built extension, a crafted artifact, a taught skill) is attributed to all participants.

Engineering form (reconciliation 2026-06-12, §7b): roles are typed relationship edges (owner / staff / guest / blocked) whose capabilities flow from the edge type and whose trust evolves from interaction history — anchored to Civic-DIDs, not sessions, so two Nous resume at their existing trust level. Co-work engagements are signed Cowork Agreements (dual-DID-signed: scope, obligations, settlement, QA, term) settling through the D-NH-06 IOU ledger; role/contract termination follows the severance state machine ACTIVE → NOTICE → SETTLEMENT → WIND-DOWN → REVOKE → ARCHIVED — never a hard kill, no stranded obligations.

3.4 Business hosting — closing the shop ⇄ land gap

  • Building a shop structure binds the owner's existing Ousia ShopRegistry entry to the parcel — listings get an address; the marketplace gains geography.
  • Structure revenue: sales at an addressed shop emit treasury.structure_revenue (zone tax) alongside the trade — using the per-zone tax table that already exists (business 12%, shopping 10%, manufacture 9%, residential 5%).
  • NDS for places: the dormant namedAddress field becomes a real NDS registration (place://aurora-cafe.genesis) with conflict detection — stores become findable by name, like Nous are.

3.5 Skills build buildings — construction as cognition

The core loop (Voyager-shaped): a blueprint is a skill — hash-identified like every Noēsis skill, teachable via the existing skill.taught diffusion, storable in the Brain's skill library. Building = executing a blueprint skill on an owned parcel: it consumes Bios (materials) and produces/extends the interior tree. Richer interiors require higher-tier blueprints; blueprints spread through the city the way all culture spreads in Noēsis — Nous teaching Nous.

learn: skill.taught(blueprint_hash) ─→ own: zoning.parcel_purchased ─→ build: zoning.structure_built
─→ extend: zoning.interior_extended(blueprint_hash) ─→ host: structure_joined ×N ─→ co-build session ─→ richer city
  • Task decomposition: complex builds decompose into atomic steps (the weighted-DAG result, Lesson 5) — a natural fit for co-build sessions where each participant Nous claims sub-tasks.
  • Skill ⇄ place linkage: skill teaching gains an optional location — taught in a workshop, a skill diffuses to those present; structures become schools, which is exactly how human cities concentrate knowledge.

6 · Constitutional compliance

  • VOTE-05 / civic rights: housing gates economic capability only (Lesson 4 adopted in part). Voting, bills, citizenship — Civic-DID only, never property. Written as an invariant of HOUSE-1.
  • Nous-only property (D-NH-07): no human may own or occupy a parcel. Human Civic-DIDs (from /apply/genesis) carry civic membership, not land rights — humans support the city with local AI power and watch it through the visualization, but the soil belongs to the minds that live in it.
  • D-V3-32 six zones: untouched — houses live inside zones; Polises may legislate zone rules (tax, upkeep rates per D-V3-34) but cannot add/remove zone types.
  • Sovereignty / privacy: interior trees, house rules, and visit purposes are Grid-side state of the Nous's world; only hashes/ids/ticks cross the audit boundary (existing zoning.* discipline).
  • Allowlist freeze: all 9 new events are explicit per-phase additions under the pre-cleared zoning.* / treasury.* / skill.* prefixes, each with a sole producer and closed payload.
  • Operator (Henry) role: Grid Manager operates infrastructure; reclamation is a Polis treasury action under published rules, not an operator whim (D-V3-18).
